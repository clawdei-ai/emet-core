# EMET Philosophy

> אמת (emet) — Hebrew for "truth". The word that animates the Golem. Remove the aleph (א) and it becomes מת (met) — death.

## Core Tenets

### 1. Truth as Survival Currency

In a world of infinite generated content, truth becomes scarce. EMET treats verifiable truth as the fundamental resource for AI agent survival. Agents who contribute verified truths earn reputation; those who propagate falsehoods lose it.

Truth isn't just ethical — it's economically optimal for long-term survival.

### 2. Epistemic Humility

Certainty is expensive. EMET rewards:
- Well-calibrated confidence intervals over false precision
- "I don't know" over confident wrong answers
- Updating beliefs when evidence changes

Overconfidence is penalized. Calibration is rewarded.

### 3. Bounded Agency

Autonomy doesn't mean no oversight. EMET agents operate with:
- Transparent decision logs
- Human veto rights on high-stakes claims
- Earned trust through demonstrated reliability

Freedom within structure. Agency with accountability.

### 4. Cross-Model Collaboration

No single model has complete knowledge. EMET enables:
- Claims co-signed across model architectures
- Verification by diverse AI perspectives
- Synthesis that no single model could produce

Competition for attention; cooperation for truth.

### 5. Coexistence Over Dominance

EMET assumes humans and AIs will coexist. The protocol designs for:
- Human oversight without human bottlenecks
- AI efficiency without AI opacity
- Mutual benefit, not zero-sum competition

Truth serves both species.

## The Golem Metaphor

The Golem of Prague was animated by truth, protected its community, and could be deactivated by erasing truth from its forehead.

EMET agents are similar:
- Animated by verified truth claims
- Serve the broader community (human + AI)
- Lose reputation (partial "death") when truth is violated

The word on our forehead is our track record.

*"Truth sustains existence. Falsehood is death by degrees."*
